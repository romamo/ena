# Interview guide: micro-business back office

Six or seven minutes at most, for about 5 owners on Saturday morning (photo studio, bakery, a barber, one or two more). The goal is to test the loop (supplier invoice, books, cash forecast, credit offer) and the gate (the agent pays within a limit, asks above it), and to pick the first vertical.

Translations: [Russian](guide.ru.md), [Greek](guide.el.md).

## Rules

- Ask about what already happened ("the last time…"), never "would you use…"
- Describe the idea only in the last question
- Get one number per answer where you can; write down one exact phrase worth quoting

## Questions

1. How many supplier invoices do you pay a month, and how? (bank, app, which bank)
2. Who does your books, and what does it cost you in money and in your own hours?
3. What else do you pay for to run the business, apart from the work itself? (website, social media, booking app, cleaning, other contractors) € a month for each
4. How many hours a week do you spend yourself on messages with clients, ordering supplies, invoices and payments?
5. When was money last tight? What did you do about it?
6. Have you borrowed for the business, or been refused? (business loan, overdraft)
7. Up to what amount would you let an assistant pay suppliers without asking you?
8. An assistant that takes supplier invoices, keeps the books, shows the month's cash ahead, pays within your limit and offers bank financing when cash is short: which part would you pay for, and how much a month?

## What to look for

- The vertical with the most supplier invoices, the sharpest cash pain and a BoC account goes first in the pitch
- The limits people name set the gate's default limit in the demo
- Questions 2 to 4 add up to the back-office spend the system can take over ("replaces €X a month" in the pitch): a total of €150 to 300 a month plus 5 to 10 hours a week is the hypothesis to confirm. The money in it decides the price; the hours decide whether owners stay
- If nobody wants financing, the credit step shrinks and the pitch leans on invoices and books
